# Section 148 Reassessment Notice: CA Response Workflow

If your client received a Section 148 notice in the last two years, the first question is not "what income escaped?" — it is "was this notice even validly issued?" The 2021 amendments completely rewired the reassessment framework, and procedural defects now carry real jurisdictional consequences. Here is everything you need to know to protect your client from the moment the notice lands.

---

## The 2021 Overhaul: What Changed in Section 147–151

The Finance Act 2021 dismantled the old reassessment architecture and replaced it with a tighter, information-driven system. The familiar "reason to believe" standard is gone. An AO can now initiate reassessment only when there is "information suggesting that income has escaped assessment," and that information must come from a CBDT-prescribed source — not the AO's own suspicion or internal file review.

The most significant addition is Section 148A, which mandates a pre-notice inquiry and show-cause procedure before any formal reassessment notice can be issued. This is not a procedural nicety — it is a jurisdictional gate. Skip it, and the Section 148 notice is vulnerable to challenge.

The time limits were restructured too. For escaped income below ₹50 lakh, the AO is confined to three years from the end of the relevant assessment year. Above ₹50 lakh, the limit stretches to ten years, but the approval hierarchy is stricter, requiring sanction from the PCIT or CCIT. Section 151A additionally empowers CBDT to route reassessment through a centralised faceless unit, which is now operational for most cases.

---

## Ashish Agarwal Ruling Decoded: Impact on Pending Notices

The Supreme Court's May 2022 judgment in *Union of India v. Ashish Agarwal* addressed a specific crisis: tens of thousands of reassessment notices issued between 1 April 2021 and 30 June 2021 under the old regime after the new law had already come into force. The Court did not quash these notices wholesale. Instead, it treated them as notices issued under the new Section 148A(b) and directed AOs to supply the underlying "information and material" to each assessee within 30 days.

Assessees were then given a fresh window to raise objections under Section 148A(d) before any formal Section 148 notice could proceed. This gave clients a second bite at the apple — but only if their CA acted promptly. Many clients and CAs missed this window or treated the revival as merely administrative, which was a costly mistake.

**Client Scenario:**

A manufacturing client in Ahmedabad received a Section 148 notice in May 2021 for AY 2018-19, alleging unexplained cash deposits of ₹38 lakh. The notice was issued under the old framework. Post-Ashish Agarwal, the AO reissued the information packet in July 2022. The CA reviewed the "information" and discovered it was nothing more than a third-party AIR entry that had already been addressed during the original scrutiny assessment under Section 143(3). The CA filed structured objections under Section 148A(b), raised the change-of-opinion defence, and the AO was forced to drop the reassessment at the Section 148A(d) stage — saving the client from a full assessment proceeding. The key was having the original assessment records ready and recognising the jurisdictional angle immediately.

The practical lesson from Ashish Agarwal for AY 2024-25 and AY 2025-26 cases is this: always audit whether your client received a compliant Section 148A(d) speaking order before a Section 148 notice was issued. If that order is absent or non-speaking, you have a strong foundation for a High Court writ.

## Old vs New Reassessment Regime: Side-by-Side Comparison

| Parameter | Pre-2021 Regime | Post-2021 Regime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger standard | "Reason to believe" — AO could form own view | "Information in possession" from CBDT-prescribed source only |
| Pre-notice procedure | None — AO could issue Section 148 notice directly | Mandatory Section 148A inquiry and show-cause with 7–30 day response window |
| Time limit for reopening | Up to six years from end of assessment year | Three years (below ₹50 lakh); ten years (above ₹50 lakh) |
| Approval authority | JCIT/ACIT sanction for most cases | PCIT or CCIT mandatory for high-value and extended-period cases |
| Jurisdiction | Jurisdictional Assessing Officer | Faceless Reassessment Unit under Section 151A for most cases |
| AO discretion | Broader — opinion-based reopening possible | Restricted — change of opinion expressly impermissible |

Keep this table beside you when you first review any reassessment notice. It tells you immediately which regime applies and, therefore, which defects are available to you.

## CA Response Workflow: From Notice Receipt to Order

Do not let your team treat a Section 148 notice as a routine compliance item to be filed away. Each step below is a decision point with legal consequences.

1. Verify the notice for technical validity. Check that it carries a valid digital signature, the correct DIN, the issuing authority's designation, and the right assessment year. A notice without a DIN is non-est under the CBDT circular — record this in writing immediately.
2. Obtain the information packet from the AO. Under the post-2021 framework, you are entitled to the "information and material" on which the AO proposes to rely. Request it formally in writing. If the AO fails to supply it within 30 days, document that failure — it is a procedural ground for challenge.
3. File structured objections under Section 148A(b). Your reply must address at least four layers: jurisdiction (correct AO or faceless unit?), limitation (is the notice within the three-year or ten-year window?), adequacy of information (is it credible and from a prescribed source?), and merits (does the alleged income actually exist?). Obtain a written acknowledgment of filing.
4. Evaluate the Section 148A(d) order the moment it arrives. This order must be a speaking order — it must address each objection you raised. If it dismisses your objections in a single line or is entirely silent on jurisdiction, note that ground for a potential writ. Do not simply accept it and move to the next stage.
5. File a return under Section 148 or formally note non-requirement. If income disclosed in the original return already covers the AO's allegation, record that position in writing. If a fresh return is needed, file it within the prescribed period and retain the acknowledgment.
6. Respond to Section 142(1) notices and assessment proceedings. Build a compliance calendar from the date of the Section 148 notice. Track every Section 142(1) deadline, maintain a document register, and prepare factual submissions that mirror the objections already raised at the Section 148A(b) stage.
7. Evaluate appellate and writ remedies before the assessment order is passed. If the Section 148A(d) order is procedurally defective, a writ to the High Court filed before submission to assessment jurisdiction preserves your client's rights far better than waiting for an assessment order and then appealing to CIT(A). Discuss this with counsel early.

## Jurisdictional Objections: Grounds CAs Must Evaluate

Before you engage on merits, always exhaust jurisdictional challenges. If the procedure under Section 148A was skipped entirely — no show-cause, no order under Section 148A(d) — the Section 148 notice is void ab initio. Multiple High Courts have taken this view consistently, and you should raise it at the earliest opportunity rather than waiving it by filing a return without protest.

Sanction from the wrong authority is equally fatal in high-value cases. Where escaped income exceeds ₹50 lakh and the extended ten-year window is invoked, sanction must come from the PCIT or CCIT. If it was obtained from a JCIT or ACIT, the notice cannot be sustained. Pull the sanction document and verify the designation before doing anything else.

The "stale information" objection is one of the most underused tools in the CA's kit. If the information the AO is relying on — a specific bank transaction, a third-party statement, an AIR data point — was already available and examined during the original assessment or an earlier scrutiny, it does not qualify as fresh information under the amended Section 148. Recycled information is not a valid trigger.

Finally, the change-of-opinion bar continues to apply under the new regime, even though the language changed. If the AO looked at the same set of facts during assessment, formed a view, and now wants to revisit that view without any genuinely new external information, the reassessment is impermissible. Your Section 148A(b) objections must make this argument explicitly, backed by references to the original assessment order.

## Critical Timelines and Limitation Traps to Track

The Section 148A(b) response window is deceptively short. The minimum is seven days from receipt of the notice, and the AO has discretion to extend it up to 30 days. Log the exact date the notice was received — not the date your client forwarded it to you, but the actual date of service — because the clock starts there. Missing this window means your objections may not be considered, and you lose the strongest pre-assessment defence.

The AO's obligation to pass the Section 148A(d) order is also time-bound. An unreasonably delayed order is itself a procedural irregularity, and if the delay is severe, it may indicate the AO does not have a sustainable basis for proceeding. Track this date carefully — if the order is not forthcoming, a writ application can be fashioned around the AO's inaction.

After the Section 148A(d) order is passed, the AO has only three months to issue the formal Section 148 notice. A notice issued beyond that window is time-barred and can be challenged. Set a reminder for this deadline the moment the Section 148A(d) order arrives, because AOs sometimes let this slip and it is a clean limitation ground.

For writ petitions challenging Section 148A(d) orders, file before you participate in the assessment proceedings. Courts have on occasion held that participating in the assessment without protest amounts to waiver of the jurisdictional objection. Advise your client clearly, get instructions in writing, and coordinate with counsel before any assessment hearing date.

## Practical Checklist: Documents and Data a CA Must Gather

Start building your document file the day the notice arrives — do not wait until the Section 148A(b) reply deadline is a week away. The original assessment order for the relevant year, along with all scrutiny notices and prior correspondence, is your baseline. It establishes what the AO already knew and examined, which is the foundation of your change-of-opinion and stale-information defences.

Cross-verify the AO's alleged escaped-income figure against the client's ITR acknowledgment, Form 26AS, AIS, and TIS for that year. CBDT's risk-management systems and third-party data often flag anomalies that are already fully explained by the return — you need to demonstrate this mismatch concretely, not just assert it. This cross-verification also helps you spot if the "information" is simply an auto-generated data flag that the AO has not independently verified.

Gather the primary source documents that directly rebut the specific allegation in the Section 148A notice. If the allegation is unexplained share capital, get the share application forms, bank statements, and PAN details of subscribers. If it is unaccounted cash deposits, get the bank statements and the ledger entries explaining the source. Generic explanations do not work — the reply must match the allegation point for point.

Finally, pull every Section 142(1), Section 143(2), and assessment-related document from the original proceedings. If the AO issued a questionnaire during original scrutiny that specifically covered the same transaction now being questioned, that questionnaire and the response you filed are your strongest evidence that the issue was examined and concluded. Without those records, you are arguing from memory — with them, you are arguing from the AO's own file.

---

## Your Next Step

If a Section 148 or Section 148A notice has arrived for any client this week, do not let 48 hours pass without completing three actions: verify the DIN and issuing authority, request the information and material packet in writing, and pull the original assessment records for that year. The reassessment game is won or lost in the first 15 days — the CA who moves fast controls the process.
