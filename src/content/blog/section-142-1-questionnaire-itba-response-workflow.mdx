## [Faceless Assessment](/blog/nfac-itba-portal-errors-faceless-assessment-fix/): Handle a 142(1) Questionnaire in ITBA

A Section 142(1) notice is one of the most document-intensive triggers in an income tax assessment. Under the Faceless Assessment regime, the pressure is higher because everything happens through ITBA — no AO call to clarify, no informal extension over the phone. Get the workflow wrong and a perfectly defensible client position collapses on procedural grounds.

This article walks you through the full response cycle, from reading the questionnaire to closing out your engagement file.

---

### What Is a Section 142(1) Notice Under Faceless Assessment?

Section 142(1) of the Income Tax Act empowers the Assessing Officer to call for accounts, documents, or a return before completing assessment. It is a preliminary tool — the AO is gathering information, not yet making additions. Do not treat it lightly because of that.

Under Faceless Assessment, these notices are system-generated by the National Faceless Assessment Centre (NaFAC) and routed entirely through the ITBA e-Proceedings portal. There is no direct officer contact, no physical delivery, and no informal heads-up. The notice appears in the assessee's e-Proceedings dashboard and an email or SMS alert fires as a secondary confirmation.

One point that trips up practitioners every cycle: **the 15-day response window starts from the date of issue shown in ITBA**, not from when your client forwarded you the email alert. Always log in to ITBA the moment a 142(1) alert arrives and record the actual issue date. For AY 2024-25 assessments currently in progress, NaFAC is actively issuing these notices across multiple sectors, so your response time is already ticking.

---

### Anatomy of a Clause-Wise 142(1) Questionnaire

A 142(1) questionnaire is rarely a single question. In practice, these documents run to 10–40 numbered clauses spanning financial statements, TDS reconciliation, related-party transactions, purchase creditors, and specific deductions claimed. Each clause cites the document type required, the assessment year it relates to, and the ITR schedule it maps to.

High-risk clauses — large cash deposits, unexplained credits, foreign remittances — are often flagged with a warning indicator in the ITBA system. Prioritise these first. A delayed or vague response to a flagged clause is the fastest route to an adverse assessment order.

**Case Scenario:**

A manufacturing client received a 40-clause 142(1) questionnaire for AY 2024-25. Clauses 1–12 dealt with routine financial statements and TDS reconciliation. Clauses 13–19 covered purchases from a flagged supplier. Clauses 20–28 probed a ₹3.2 crore unsecured loan from a group company. Clauses 29–40 demanded export realisation certificates and Form 15CA/CB details for three overseas transactions.

The CA team mapped clause dependencies on day one: the loan confirmations (Clauses 20–28) required the same balance sheet extract as Clauses 1–3. By identifying this overlap early, they avoided uploading contradictory figures and reduced the total document preparation time by nearly 40%. Clause dependencies are not obvious from a first read — build your tracker before you build your file.

### Document-Mapping Matrix: Clause to Evidence

Use this table to map each common clause category to the exact documents you need to gather. Note the format requirements — ITBA accepts PDF files up to 5 MB per file and ZIP archives for bulk uploads.

| Clause Category | Source Documents Required | CA Certification Needed? | Explanation Note Required? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bank statement reconciliation | Bank statements (all accounts), ledger extract, Form 26AS | No — direct upload | Yes, if credits exceed ₹1 lakh per transaction |
| Purchase creditor verification | Purchase invoices, GSTR-2A/2B reconciliation, ledger | No | Yes, if supplier is flagged |
| TDS reconciliation | Form 26AS, Form 16A, books TDS receivable ledger | No | Only if mismatch exceeds ₹10,000 |
| Depreciation schedule | Fixed asset register, addition invoices, prior-year Schedule II workings | CA certification preferred | Yes, if method change or impairment involved |
| Unsecured loans / share capital | Loan confirmation letters, ITR of lender, bank entry proof | No | Yes — mandatory for all loan clauses |
| Related-party transactions | TP documentation, Form 3CEB, agreements, invoices | Yes — CA/TP auditor sign-off | Yes |
| Export proceeds realisation | FIRC/BRC, Form 15CA/CB, shipping bills, AD bank certificate | No | Yes, if proceeds are overdue |
| Deductions claimed (80IC, 80G, etc.) | Approval letters, donation receipts, computation, relevant certificate | CA certification preferred | Yes, with clause-specific working |

Where the table flags "CA certification preferred," treat that as mandatory if the clause carries a ⚠ indicator in ITBA. Uploading uncertified documents against a high-risk clause hands the AO a reason to call for further information.

For files exceeding 5 MB, compress using standard PDF compression tools or split by sub-clause. ITBA does not warn you when an upload fails silently due to file size — always verify the upload status on screen before moving to the next clause.

### Step-by-Step Workflow to Respond via ITBA e-Proceedings

This is the sequence that works. Do not skip steps or reorder them — the portal logic depends on you following this path.

1. Log in to ITBA using your authorised representative credentials. Navigate to **e-Proceedings → View Notices**, locate the Section 142(1) notice, open it, and record the exact issue date and response deadline in your engagement tracker.
2. Download the questionnaire PDF immediately and create an internal clause tracker spreadsheet with columns for clause number, document required, responsible team member, document status, and upload confirmation. Assign owners before the end of day one.
3. Prepare a clause-wise written response note (in Word or PDF) for each clause. Keep the naming convention consistent: **PAN\_AY\_ClauseNo\_DocType** — for example, `ABCDE1234F_AY2425_Cl14_PurchaseInvoice.pdf`. This naming standard makes it easy to track and reference each file post-submission.
4. In ITBA, click **Submit Response** under the relevant notice. Select the specific clause from the dropdown, upload attachments one file per clause, and enter your textual explanation in the remarks box. Do not use the remarks box for generic language — write a clause-specific summary of what you are submitting and why.
5. After uploading all clauses, generate the **Acknowledgement Reference Number (ARN)** from the submission screen and save it immediately. This is your primary proof of compliance; treat it like a challan.
6. Open the submission summary report within ITBA and verify that no clause still shows a **Pending** status. A single pending clause means the AO receives an incomplete response — fix it before the deadline lapses, not after.
7. Save all uploaded files, the ARN, the clause tracker, and the ITBA submission summary into the client's engagement folder. This is your audit trail if the assessment is later reopened or appealed.

### Common Errors CAs Make — and How to Avoid Them

The single most damaging mistake is uploading one combined PDF covering multiple clauses. ITBA logs the submission against only one clause — the others remain marked as unanswered. Always upload a separate document set for each clause, even if it means splitting a financial statement into clause-specific extracts.

File size is a hidden trap. The 5 MB per-file limit is enforced silently — the portal does not always throw a visible error message. Compress or split every file before upload, and confirm the upload status on screen before clicking through to the next clause.

Generic cover letters are a compliance risk, not a compliance solution. If your response to a ₹50 lakh unsecured loan clause is a single paragraph saying "documents enclosed," expect a follow-up notice or worse, a best-judgement addition under Section 144. Write clause-specific explanations that mirror the AO's question and reference the documents you have attached.

Finally, do not assume an extension is automatic if you cannot gather documents in time. You must formally apply through ITBA before the deadline. Sitting on an incomplete file and planning to explain later is not a strategy — it is grounds for penalty under Section 272A.

---

### Requesting a Time Extension or Adjournment Within ITBA

If you genuinely cannot compile a complete response within 15 days — third-party confirmations are delayed, books are with a concurrent auditor — you must request an adjournment formally through ITBA. Do this at least 2–3 days before the deadline, not on the last day.

Inside e-Proceedings, use the **Request Adjournment** option linked to the specific notice. The request must state concrete reasons. "Documents are being compiled" will be rejected. "Form 15CA/CB from the AD bank is pending due to bank processing timelines" will likely be accepted. Specificity is your credibility.

ITBA auto-routes the request to the AO, but do not assume approval is coming. Monitor the notice status actively — approvals or rejections typically come within 48 hours. If the extension is denied, you are still obligated to file a response on time. Submit what you have, clearly note in the remarks which clauses have pending documents and why, and flag the expected timeline for completing those documents.

A denied extension with a partial but documented response is a far stronger position than a missed deadline with no filing at all.

---

### Post-Submission Checklist and Client Communication Template

Once you submit, the engagement does not close — it enters a monitoring phase. Follow this sequence to close the loop properly.

1. Within 24 hours of submission, confirm the ARN receipt, download the ITBA submission summary PDF, and store both in your document management system under the client's AY 2024-25 assessment folder.
2. Review the ITBA notice dashboard at 7 days and again at 10 days post-submission. The AO may schedule a further e-hearing or issue a follow-up notice — missing this window makes you look unresponsive even after a compliant submission.
3. Send the client a concise update email within 48 hours of submission. Cover the following: total clauses responded to, key documents submitted, the ARN, and a plain-English explanation of what happens next — whether that is a scrutiny assessment order, a further query round, or closure.
4. Set a calendar reminder for 30 days post-submission to log into ITBA and check for assessment orders or additional Section 142(1) rounds. AY 2025-26 assessments are already in early stages for some taxpayers, and overlapping notice cycles for different years on the same PAN are increasingly common.

### Your Next Step

If you have an active 142(1) questionnaire sitting in any client's ITBA portal right now, open the e-Proceedings dashboard today, record the exact response deadline, and build your clause tracker before anything else. The document gathering and upload can run in parallel — but without a tracker, you will miss clauses, duplicate uploads, and hand the AO an easy reason to escalate.

If your firm handles more than five concurrent faceless assessments, invest half a day this week in building a standard clause-to-document matrix for your most common industry verticals. The time saved across even one assessment cycle will more than justify it.
